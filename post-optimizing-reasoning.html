From Simulating Speech to Optimizing Reasoning

Updated 11 November 2026

Reading notes on the 2026 latent-reasoning literature.

AI narration · title and article text

Chain-of-thought lets a model write intermediate steps before answering. Those tokens can improve performance and provide material for checking a result. They are also an incomplete record of the computation that produced them. Recent work asks which parts of reasoning can be steered, compressed or represented without committing to a full sentence at every step.

What interventions can establish

He and colleagues use sparse autoencoders to identify latent features associated with reasoning behaviour. Steering a small number of features can induce CoT-like behaviour without an explicit CoT prompt, while suppressing them can impair performance. That provides causal evidence for the features in the tested models. It does not show that intermediate text is unnecessary for every task or that reasoning is independent of language. [1⁠]

Turpin and colleagues show that biasing features in a prompt can alter an answer without appearing in the explanation. Anthropic extends that approach to reasoning models and six hint types. The published averages are 25% hint acknowledgement for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and 39% for DeepSeek R1 when the hint influenced the answer. Some individual settings fall below 20%; there is no universal 20% ceiling. Outcome-based RL initially improves acknowledgement in their training experiment and then plateaus. [2⁠, 4⁠]

The experiment tests disclosure of a known influence. A useful monitor should therefore be checked against controlled interventions, instead of assuming that a plausible explanation includes every cause of the answer. Conversely, a missing disclosure cannot establish that all visible reasoning is causally irrelevant.

Pfau, Merrill and Bowman train transformers on controlled algorithmic tasks where repeated filler tokens provide useful additional computation steps. That demonstrates a role for sequential computation beyond the semantic content of the trace in those tasks. It does not establish that filler can replace mathematical working in a general-purpose model. [3⁠]

Keeping several next steps in one vector

Multiplex Thinking samples several candidate next tokens and combines their embeddings into a continuous multiplex token. A confident distribution approaches an ordinary discrete step; an uncertain distribution can retain several candidates without adding sequence positions. On-policy RL trains these trajectories. Tang and colleagues report shorter sequences and gains against their discrete-CoT and RL baselines on mathematics benchmarks. [5⁠]

This changes the representation of the intermediate computation. It is not literal quantum superposition, and a mixture of embeddings need not preserve every property of separate search branches. The interesting comparison is whether the method improves success at matched inference cost, including the candidate sampling and training it requires.

Compact plans that remain verbalizable

Fast-ThinkAct distils textual reasoning into compact latent plans for vision-language-action policies. The representation deliberately remains verbalizable: a decoder can translate it back into language. Huang and colleagues report up to 89.3% lower inference latency than their reasoning-VLA baselines, with results on manipulation and reasoning tasks. The mechanism is useful evidence for compressing a planning interface while retaining a language link, rather than evidence that language never contributed to reasoning. [6⁠]

For manufacturing, this suggests distilling repeated planning into a small, fast representation while evaluating the resulting actions and the decoded plan separately. A verbalizer offers an inspection interface; its faithfulness still needs its own test against what changes the action.

How much intermediate computation helps?

Wu and colleagues find an inverted-U relationship between trace length and accuracy in their settings: additional steps help up to an optimum and can hurt beyond it. The optimum depends on task difficulty and model capability. That is an argument for selecting an appropriate reasoning budget, not for concluding that useful reasoning was never in the generated steps. [7⁠]

A useful budget comparison holds the problem distribution, decoding conditions and total cost accounting fixed. It should report correct answers per token or per second alongside accuracy, and distinguish a longer sampled trace from an intervention that actually gives the model more useful computation. A claim about an optimum requires a measured cost–accuracy curve for the task and model under comparison.

Agent loops and correlated errors

ReAct combines generated reasoning and actions with observations returned by an environment. The runtime executes tool calls, enforces permissions and returns results; the model uses those observations to choose subsequent actions. This feedback loop can solve tasks that a single ungrounded completion cannot. Calling it a loop does not settle how competent its policy is. [8⁠]

Constrained decoding can enforce JSON or a grammar during generation. A missing comma is therefore an avoidable interface failure, not a fundamental limitation of tool use. Schema-valid requests can still choose the wrong tool, use incorrect arguments or misinterpret an observation. Those semantic failures need validators and task-level checks. [9⁠]

Yang and colleagues study diminishing returns when agents produce highly correlated evidence. Their heterogeneity includes models, prompts and tools, not just different model weights. Additional calls from one model can help, but agent count alone does not determine the gain. The relevant experiment compares complementary information and cost against an equally resourced single-agent baseline. [10⁠]

Reward the property being monitored

VERITAS adds turn-level rewards for faithfulness in agentic search, distinguishing links between reasoning, search, retrieved information and the final answer. It improves the authors' measured faithfulness and task performance against outcome-only training. Those metrics concern evidence traceability in retrieval-augmented generation; they do not certify disclosure of every internal influence or predict a particular curve against a reasoning-effort slider. [11⁠]

Latent steering, multiplex tokens and distilled plans offer different ways to use intermediate computation. Their practical value can be tested through task success, latency, training cost and response to interventions. For a process-control application, the decisive comparison is whether the faster plan still chooses a valid action and exposes enough evidence to diagnose a failure.
