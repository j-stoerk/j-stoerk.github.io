01
RL-Menu Agent Learns to Fold by Seat

Developed through ten iterations, frodo combines a reinforcement-learned menu policy with hand-coded strategies and peaked at rank 5 with a rating of 2302.8. Failed negotiations often scored near the field median, favoring aggressive demands when the agent controlled the final offer. The same stance from the weaker seat caused the largest measured losses. The RL layer discovered this seat distinction; distilled heuristics then outperformed the learner that found it.

Question
Which bargaining decisions improve the payoff percentile against a changing field?
Approach
A reinforcement-learned menu selects among hand-coded strategy primitives. Seat-aware heuristics are distilled from controlled comparisons.
Evidence and scope
GLEE competition runs and game-specific ablations. Peak rank and rating describe a particular live field, rather than general agent competence.

02
Interference and retention in continual learning

Learning a new task can overwrite a model's earlier knowledge. I model this forgetting as weight-interference energy under a task's feature covariance and derive a replay-free controller (IGFA) that protects earlier tasks while sharing capacity where directions do not conflict.

Question
Which directions of a new update damage an earlier task, and how much learning remains after protecting them?
Approach
Interference-gated functional allocation shares or projects updates according to their alignment with protected task directions.
Evidence and scope
Rotated-Digits with a frozen backbone and five seeds. The exact quadratic argument assumes squared loss and stationarity; adapting encoders require a separate evaluation.

03
Capturing the calendering U-shape in lithium-ion electrode thermal conductivity

Porosity-only and static-contact models miss the conductivity dip during calendering. I develop a differentiable Zehner–Bauer–Schlünder closure that captures this dip and tests whether contact-network growth or particle reorientation dominates through-plane thermal transport.

Question
Why can electrode conductivity fall during early calendering before recovering at greater compaction?
Approach
A process-dependent contact term extends a Knudsen-corrected Zehner–Bauer–Schlünder closure. Graphite reorientation is examined as a competing mechanism.
Evidence and scope
27 states in four graphite and NMC families: 4.5% in-sample MAPE and 7.8% leave-one-state-out error. Conductivity alone does not identify the separate shares of contact evolution and reorientation.
